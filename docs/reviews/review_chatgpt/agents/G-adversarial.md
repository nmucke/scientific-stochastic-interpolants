# Stage 1 G — Adversarial ICLR review

**Recommendation: Reject.** The formal unification is potentially valuable, but the current submission has one flagship experiment that tests a materially different problem from the one claimed, a practical likelihood construction whose central “covariance” interpretation is not guaranteed, and a novelty argument that omits or misstates the closest current literature. The first issue cannot be repaired in rebuttal without rerunning the urban study.

## Attack 1 — The urban experiment observes the variable claimed to be unobserved

- **Location** — Section 5.3 and Figure 5, pp. 8–9: “temperature is inferred only through its coupling to the observed flow.”
- **Severity** — **blocking**.
- **Problem** — The executed urban mask samples all four channels `(u,v,w,theta_l)`, not velocity alone: the saved headline masks contain 111 and 256 direct temperature observations. The sensor budget is also computed from all four full-grid channels and then placed in fluid cells, giving actual fluid-DOF densities of about 1.230% and 2.459%, rather than the stated 0.78125% and 1.5625%; Table 2 and Figures 5/15–21 therefore evaluate a different, easier inference problem.
- **Confidence** — **high**. `paper_experiments/cases/urban/driver.py:307–321` builds a four-channel operator, `paper_experiments/cases/urban/_urban_pipeline.py:147–176` samples from the four-channel fluid mask using the full-grid denominator, and the two saved SI-SDE headline masks have per-channel counts `[136,129,136,111]` and `[243,243,282,256]`. No further check is needed.
- **Classification** — **fixable only by experiment**; it is fatal to the current urban interpretation and all claims about temperature inferred from velocity alone.
- **Fix** — Restrict candidate observations to channels 0–2, define the requested percentage against fluid velocity DOFs, rerun every urban method/variant at both densities, and regenerate Table 2 and Figures 5/15–21. A corrected single-rollout rerun is roughly one GPU-day; completing the intended five-rollout comparison at the same time is approximately 5–6 GPU-days on one 24-GiB GPU.

**Hostile review.** The most realistic experiment does not test the scientific question advertised. The manuscript repeatedly calls temperature “unobserved” and attributes reconstruction to velocity–temperature coupling, yet the evaluation directly supplies temperature measurements and uses substantially denser masks than reported. This is not a caption error: it changes the conditional distribution, invalidates the cross-variable inference story, and contaminates every urban ranking and qualitative figure. Because the urban case is the only long, multivariable demonstration, I cannot treat the empirical validation as reliable.

**Authors’ rebuttal.** We agree that the observation operator was constructed over all four normalized channels while the prose described velocity-only sensing. The mask is shared by every method, so the within-task comparison remains fair, and the experiment still demonstrates sparse multivariable assimilation. We will correct the wording and add a velocity-only rerun in the revision.

**Assessment of rebuttal.** **Not convincing.** Shared access to the wrong observations preserves within-task parity but does not support the claimed task or the conclusion that temperature is recovered through coupling. A promised post-submission rerun cannot establish whether the rankings, uncertainty, or long-rollout behavior survive under velocity-only sensing; this attack lands and drives rejection.

## Attack 2 — The practical “Gaussian covariance” need not be a covariance

- **Location** — Section 4.3, Eq. (17), p. 5: “The implemented covariances are”.
- **Severity** — **major**.
- **Problem** — The exact source moment is symmetric positive semidefinite because it is a true covariance, but substituting a learned score/drift Jacobian and damping it as in Eq. (17) preserves neither symmetry nor positive semidefiniteness. The active field configurations disable the available symmetrize/eigenvalue-floor option and solve the raw matrix, so the object inverted in Eq. (15) need not define either a Gaussian covariance or a Gaussian likelihood score.
- **Confidence** — **high** on the missing guarantee: the networks impose no conservative-field/symmetric-Jacobian constraint, Eq. (17) does not symmetrize, `sigma_bar_eig_floor: false` is active, and `src/scisi/likelihood_models/gaussian_likelihood.py:458–471` performs the raw solve. Logging antisymmetric norms and minimum eigenvalues on headline trajectories would quantify how often the theoretical possibility occurs, but is not needed to establish the false literal interpretation.
- **Classification** — **fixable by text change**, but only by relinquishing the literal covariance/Gaussian-score claim; retaining that interpretation requires a code change and field reruns.
- **Fix** — Replace the lead-in to Eq. (17) with: “For inexact learned fields, the following damped operators need not remain symmetric positive semidefinite and are used as empirical guidance preconditioners rather than literal covariances.” Add the same limitation in Section 6. If a literal covariance is essential, symmetrize and project to the PSD cone, then rerun all shared-covariance field results (multi-GPU-day cost).

**Hostile review.** The practical contribution is sold as a closed-form Gaussian likelihood with a source covariance that carries prior correlations, but the implemented learned approximation can be nonsymmetric or indefinite and is inverted anyway. Damping is not a PSD guarantee, and finite numerical output is not evidence that this is a covariance or a score field. Thus the exact theorem and moment identities do not justify the actual shared-Jacobian sampler used for the strongest sparse-observation results; at best this is an empirically chosen, nonconservative preconditioner whose behavior is uncharacterized.

**Authors’ rebuttal.** Lemma 4.3 proves that the exact expression is a covariance, and the practical section already labels learned Jacobians, damping, and stopped covariance gradients as approximations. The positive observation-noise term and tuned damping made all reported solves stable, while the analytical case verifies the exact construction. We can clarify that the learned operator is heuristic outside the exact-score setting.

**Assessment of rebuttal.** **Partially convincing only after the proposed text correction.** It separates the exact theorem from the learned implementation, but numerical stability does not restore symmetry, PSD, or score integrability. The rebuttal can make the paper honest by narrowing its probabilistic interpretation; it cannot retain the current literal “implemented covariances” language without diagnostics and likely reruns.

## Attack 3 — The novelty delta is stale and built on inaccurate contrasts

- **Location** — Introduction and Related Work, p. 2: “including FIG and OT-ODE”; “Closest to our work is Yan et al. (2025)”.
- **Severity** — **major**.
- **Problem** — The paper omits current close work on conditioning pretrained flows and sequential SI assimilation, including FLOWER (ICLR 2026), FlowDPS, DAISI, FAPS, and operator-valued/multitask stochastic interpolants, while treating FIG as a literal family member. Its Appendix F contrast is also materially inaccurate: published FIG has a time-dependent Gaussian measurement-interpolant likelihood and a probability-path derivation, so “uses no covariance” and “not derived from a probability path” do not describe that method.
- **Confidence** — **high** that the omissions and FIG characterization are real after checking the primary records and FIG algorithm/derivation; **medium** on absolute priority, because proving that no earlier paper derives precisely this equal-marginal family is impossible. A line-by-line comparison against the five closest methods would raise confidence in the residual novelty claim.
- **Classification** — **fixable by text change** if the narrower theoretical delta survives an explicit comparison.
- **Fix** — Replace the “closest” paragraph with: “Recent methods condition pretrained FM priors without retraining (FlowDPS; FLOWER), DAISI applies guided SI sampling in sequential DA, FAPS uses covariance-preconditioned function-space FM, and operator-valued SIs support posterior tasks after multitask pretraining. Our narrower distinction is one observation-interpolant correction and equal-marginal ODE/SDE family for conventionally trained SI, FM, and diffusion priors.” Replace “including FIG and OT-ODE” with “closely related to FIG and OT-ODE,” and correct Appendix F’s FIG description. Estimated effort: half to one day of primary-paper comparison and editing; no experiment is required.

**Hostile review.** The paper motivates itself by saying posterior samplers were developed model by model and identifies FIG as the closest work, but that literature picture is already obsolete for an ICLR 2027 submission. There are now pretrained-FM posterior solvers, a sequential SI data-assimilation method, covariance-preconditioned scientific posterior sampling, and a broader SI framework supporting posterior tasks. Worse, FIG is simultaneously claimed as a member and described using limitations its published method does not have. Without a precise comparison, I cannot determine whether the contribution is a genuinely new sampler family or a repackaging of existing observation-interpolation and likelihood-guidance ideas.

**Authors’ rebuttal.** The cited newer methods are adjacent rather than equivalent: FLOWER/FlowDPS are FM-specific inverse solvers, DAISI adds a distinct inverse-sampling filtering construction, FAPS is function-space FM, and operator-valued SIs require specialized multitask pretraining. None derives a single likelihood correction and equal-marginal ODE/SDE family spanning conventionally trained SI, FM, and diffusion priors. FIG is a deterministic analogue, not literally identical to our continuous ODE; we will revise that wording and add the missing comparisons.

**Assessment of rebuttal.** **Potentially convincing, but not in the current paper.** The proposed narrower delta is plausible and could survive, yet it must be stated and substantiated rather than asserted during rebuttal. The inaccurate FIG contrast also needs correction. This attack lands against the present positioning but is repairable without new experiments.
