# Related work and novelty audit

Research date: 2026-10-07. Primary sources only. Downloaded arXiv HTML/text and SHA-256 records for recent closest works are in `../sources/broader_literature_provenance.json`. The references below do not establish that their benchmarks or timings transfer to this project.

- [DYffusion, NeurIPS 2023](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/8df90a1440ce782d1f5607b7a38f2531-Abstract-Conference.html), Rühling Cachay et al.: learned dynamics-aware interpolation and forecasting; probabilistic evaluation on SST, fluid flow, and spring systems. Appropriate contextual reference for continuous-time and multistep forecasting. Its fluid task differs from our stochastic forcing benchmark.
- [GenCast, Nature 637, 84–90 (2025)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-08252-9), Price et al. Online publication December 2024, volume year 2025. Conditional generative weather ensembles establish the practical importance of calibration and ensemble skill; not a same-Brownian-path distillation method. Use journal year 2025 consistently.
- [Swift, arXiv:2509.25631](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25631), Stock, Arcomano, Kotamarthi: a single-step autoregressive consistency forecaster with CRPS fine-tuning. Directly prevents a claim of first single-step probabilistic weather forecast model. Read abstract/method context; do not transfer its speedup claim.
- [Recursive Flow Matching, arXiv:2605.26535](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26535), Huang, Xu, Vadgama, Yu: recursive trajectory consistency for one/few-step scientific emulation. Read methods, experiments, appendix dataset settings. Its NS benchmark is channel flow past circular obstacles (velocity/pressure), rather than randomly forced periodic vorticity. Its conditional flow formulation and few-step motivation are close prior art; the proposed distinction here is a Brownian-conditioned transition for a specified forecasting SDE. No claim of first few-step scientific forecasting is defensible.
- [PixelFlowCast, arXiv:2605.10046](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10046), Zhu et al.: few-step pixel mean-flow precipitation nowcasting. Include as further evidence that direct fast forecasting is established. Do not repeat the abstract's assertion that conditional flow matching inherently requires latent compression: this is not true in general.
- [StocBench, arXiv:2608.22309](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22309), Pfister, Holzschuh, Thuerey: especially close benchmark paper. Read benchmark, metrics, experiments, dataset appendix. It compares SI, FM, diffusion solvers, consistency distillation, and adversarial FM distillation on stochastic Kolmogorov flow. It uses simulated conditional reference ensembles and assesses enstrophy under autoregressive rollout. It also distinguishes a forcing-observed control setting. It demonstrates that few-step fluid forecasting and accuracy/cost comparisons themselves are not novel.

Proposed claim boundary: a conditional, same-path Itô-map adaptation of the point-mass forecasting SI; faithful shared Brownian coupling and explicit compressed-path limitations; scientific evidence assessing whether this added stochastic structure is useful against deterministic map baselines. This is presently an application/method adaptation, not a new general Itô-map theory. Strong evidence is required for ICML-level significance.

Useful follow-up if time permits: direct comparison to RecFM or consistency distillation using the same training resources, conditional reference ensembles from a provenance-matched physical solver, and assessment of whether Brownian resolution changes spread/spectral preservation. These should not displace the user's core comparison prematurely.

## Elementary compression observation added to manuscript

For finite Brownian features `Z`, squared-error endpoint regression learns `E[Y|Z]`; the minimum same-path error is `E Var(Y|Z)` (trace for vectors). Total covariance shows the corresponding conditional-mean map can lose spread. This does not prohibit a low-dimensional random transport from matching the endpoint distribution under another training objective. The scalar OU process `dX=-X dt+dW`, `X0=0`, gives an exact example: observing only `W1` leaves variance `0.03275595749`; four equal-subinterval increments leave `0.00223774616`. These numbers are analytical illustrations, not experiments. The manuscript's arbitrary-time bridge implementation samples the queried martingale values exactly; the approximation is missing path information entering the nonlinear drift response.
