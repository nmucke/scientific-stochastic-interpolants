\section{Implementation and Reproducibility Record}
The implementation introduces \texttt{BaseMapModel}, \texttt{FollmerItoMapModel}, \texttt{FlowMapModel}, and \texttt{MapTrainer}. Existing datasets, loaders, history conditioning, normalization, and forecast metrics are reused. The model adds a learned interval embedding to the backbone's starting-time embedding and injects Brownian feature channels through a bias-free $1\times1$ convolution after the original input block. Both added branches initialize to zero, allowing compatible pretrained drift weights to initialize the complete backbone. On the diagonal, the interval and Brownian branches vanish exactly. The Hydra train and test entry points use the existing configuration mechanism.

The implemented map family is restricted to the existing affine diffusion schedule $\gamma_t=\varepsilon(1-t)$. General deterministic diffusion schedules require a corresponding variance-clock implementation; state-dependent diffusion is outside this construction. The Navier--Stokes study uses no spatial mask. Any use of a nontrivial update mask would need to be reflected in the mathematical diffusion and drift.

\paragraph{Training interval distribution.} At maximum curriculum length $L$, sample a gap uniformly from $[0.01L,L]$ and a start time uniformly from the valid range. An additional endpoint atom uses $(s,t)=(0,L)$. The split fraction $(u-s)/(t-s)$ is uniform on $[0.25,0.75]$. Thus the sampling is not uniform over the time triangle; off-diagonal gaps below 0.01 are excluded after the curriculum, while the diagonal has its own regression loss. Stage two uses $L=1$ and endpoint-atom probability 0.5.

The experiment archive records commands, resolved configurations, seeds, data splits, software/device metadata, checkpoint hashes, selection rules, and raw evaluation outputs. \subsection{Ideal Identities and Finite-History Modeling}
\label{app:method-assumptions}
For the ideal marginal construction we assume the regular conditional-density and schedule conditions of \citet{chen2024forecasting}, not only the endpoint values listed in the main text. The map identities additionally require a specified regular version of $b_t(x;h)$ and a pathwise unique strong solution with finite second moments for the initial states under consideration. Local spatial Lipschitz continuity and appropriate growth or nonexplosion conditions on compact interior time intervals provide a familiar sufficient setting. We assume the continuity needed for the drift average to extend to $G_{t,t}=b_t$ on that interior domain. Existence of a forecast transition at time one is an additional endpoint assumption; we do not assert a continuous diagonal extension at both endpoints for every off-support state.

A nonsingular regression target alone does not imply these properties: for deterministic zero endpoints the present schedule gives $b_t(x)=-x/(1-t)$ on interior times, whose spatial Lipschitz constant diverges at one. That example is outside the positive-density setting, but explains the need to qualify endpoint statements. Learned neural drifts, numerically integrated, are practical approximations of this ideal construction; no smooth-density theorem is claimed for the empirical forecast distribution.

The physical forecast loop defines a finite-history surrogate. For its joint law to equal the true future law, the chosen history must be sufficient:
\begin{equation}
\Law(Y_{n+1}\mid Y_{\le n})
=\Law(Y_{n+1}\mid Y_{n-p+1:n}).
\end{equation}
The kernel must also be correct on generated histories, and either time-homogeneous or conditioned on relevant time dependence. Unobserved state and spatial coarsening can violate these requirements even when the fitted one-step conditional is exact. Fresh independent randomness samples the specified kernel correctly; it does not restore omitted memory. Neither the generative-time composition identity nor long-rollout boundedness proves joint physical forecast correctness.

All conditional expectations and covariance identities in Proposition~\ref{prop:compression} fix $(s,t,x,h)$. Its projection argument requires square-integrable targets and predictors. The endpoint coupling inequality similarly requires finite second moments and is not specific to this optimizer. These elementary identities diagnose approximation and information loss; they are not new convergence or calibration theorems for the implemented training loss.

\subsection{Numerical Teacher and Actual Training Objective}
\label{app:numerical-teacher}
Teacher-rollout supervision integrates the frozen neural drift by Euler steps but uses exact additive-diffusion increments:
\begin{equation}
X_{j+1}=X_j+\delta_j b_{\mathrm T}(X_j,t_j;h)
+M_{t_{j+1}}-M_{t_j}.
\label{eq:numerical-teacher}
\end{equation}
The existing stochastic-interpolant comparator instead adds $\gamma(t_j)\sqrt{\delta_j}\,\xi_j$, the left-point Euler--Maruyama diffusion rule. The networks can therefore be identical while their finite-step numerical transitions differ. For zero drift and $n$ uniform steps, exact endpoint noise variance is $\varepsilon^2/3$, whereas the left-point rule gives $\varepsilon^2[1/3+1/(2n)+1/(6n^2)]$. This calculation concerns zero drift, not predicted fluid variance. An exact-diffusion solver comparison remains a future empirical control; the solver baselines use the left-point rule throughout.

The optimized objective is $\lambda_{\mathrm{diag}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{diag}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{off}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{off}}$, with $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{off}}=\mathcal L_{\mathrm{comp}}$ in stage one and same-path average-drift teacher MSE in stage two. Since the shared martingale cancels, endpoint residual equals $(t-s)$ times average-drift residual. Consequently, unweighted average-drift MSE weights endpoint squared error by $(t-s)^{-2}$ across the sampled intervals; it is not unweighted endpoint MSE. Average-drift units align finite-time and diagonal supervision, without removing this weighting.

In the Navier--Stokes configuration of record, $\lambda_{\mathrm{diag}}=1$ and the maximum $\lambda_{\mathrm{off}}=1$. Stage one ramps the latter over 100 updates and grows maximum interval length from 0.125 to one over 500 updates. Its endpoint-atom probability is 0.25; its detached target parameters follow $\bar\theta\leftarrow0.95\bar\theta+0.05\theta$ after each update. Stage two uses unit weights immediately, full interval length and endpoint probability 0.5; its target is the frozen teacher, not its recorded EMA copy. Both stages use zero diagonal-only warmup steps. A separate warmup is an optional implementation feature, not part of this configuration.

The restricted final interval distribution excludes direct gaps below 0.01 and child gaps below 0.0025, while the diagonal is trained separately. Thus it does not enforce a refinement-to-zero identity on a complete time domain. Finite features also need not admit exact pathwise composition, even for conditional-mean teacher predictors. The EMA objective, restricted intervals and compressed representation do not meet the full-path identification assumptions of \citet{pan2026itomaps}; no identifiability, zero population residual, or forecast-law bound from PSD loss is asserted.

Composition avoids differentiating time-dependent compressed features while holding a full Brownian realization fixed. An alternative Lagrangian residual would be $G+(t-s)\partial_tG-b_t(\Phi_{s,t};h)$; deterministic Eulerian identities cannot simply be applied to a noisy starting state. Brownian independence throughout refers to increments after the interval start, before conditioning on any revealed future bridge endpoint, rather than independence of absolute future Brownian values from an adapted state.
