\section{Brownian Compression in the Frozen Fluid Teacher}\label{app:ns-compression}
We estimated how much endpoint variation the finite Brownian features omit, independently of training a neural map. Fix validation trajectory 160 at origin 45 and its five-field history. For each feature count $K\in\{1,4,16\}$, we sampled eight coarse paths and four independent finer Brownian bridge realizations conditional on each coarse path. The frozen SI teacher integrated its drift using 64 Euler steps with exact martingale increments. Cached coarse observations were copied into independent bridge forks; new fine values in a fork were not fed back into its parent. Thus replicas share the prescribed coarse features and differ only in unobserved path details.

Let $X_{gr}$ be an endpoint for coarse group $g$ and replica $r$, and let $\bar X_g$ be its group mean. Compute coordinatewise sample variances and then average over the state coordinates:
\begin{align}
 \widehat W&=\frac1G\sum_{g=1}^G
 \left\langle\operatorname{Var}_{r}(X_{gr})\right\rangle,\\
 \widehat B&=\left\langle\operatorname{Var}_{g}(\bar X_g)\right\rangle,
 \qquad G=8,\quad R=4.
\end{align}
All sample variances use their unbiased denominators. The first statistic estimates the conditional-variance term in Proposition~\ref{prop:compression} for this fixed history and numerical teacher. Finite replication inflates the variance of group means by $\widehat W/R$, so we estimate explained variance as $\widehat B-\widehat W/R$ and total variance as $\widehat B+(1-1/R)\widehat W$.

The estimated omitted variances were $0.05596$, $0.01711$, and $0.00106$ for $K=1,4,16$, respectively. Their standard errors across the eight coarse groups were $0.00379$, $0.00189$, and $0.000094$. The corresponding omitted fractions of estimated total endpoint variance were 28.43\%, 9.68\%, and 0.66\%. Conditioning on sixteen rather than four increments reduced the estimated omitted variance by a factor of 16.2 (Figure~\ref{fig:ns-compression}). These observations motivated a controlled feature-resolution ablation.

This diagnostic uses only one history and a small nested Monte Carlo sample. True total conditional forecast variance is independent of $K$, whereas its estimates varied from 0.161 to 0.197; the fractions therefore have substantial Monte Carlo uncertainty. The standard errors do not quantify variation across histories, and pixels are not treated as independent replicates. The teacher itself is learned and numerically integrated, and its 64-step diagnostic differs from the 32-step supervision and the original SI sampler's Euler--Maruyama increments. The numbers cannot be subtracted directly from aggregate student validation losses.

Finally, this is an information limit for squared-error prediction under the specified Brownian coupling. It is neither a lower bound on CRPS nor a guarantee that increasing $K$ improves trained-map forecasts. The finite-feature PSD objective also need not have the same optimum as conditional-mean endpoint regression. In particular, the estimated $K=4$ conditional-mean predictor retains about 95\% of the teacher's standard deviation here, so this compression mechanism alone does not explain all losses of spread observed in the initial student.
