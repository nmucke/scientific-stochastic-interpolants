\subsection{Pixel-Space Video Forecasting}\label{sec:video-main}
We also evaluate KTH human-action videos and CLEVRER object-motion videos, using FVD as in \citet{chen2024forecasting}. This pilot predicts $64\times64$ pixels directly and conditions on the last $N$ consecutive frames. KTH uses grayscale frames with $N=10$ observations and $30$ forecasts; CLEVRER uses RGB, $N=2$, and $14$ forecasts at stride five. All four model families are trained separately on each dataset. The stochastic-interpolant and flow-matching teachers start from scratch; each map is initialized and trained from its own newly trained teacher. The selected models use the same 128 frozen test clips and four sampled completions per clip. FVD embeds the observed prefix together with the future, using a fixed pretrained I3D detector. Our pooled comparison has 128 real and 512 generated clips, versus 256 and 25,600 in the original study; it is a smaller pixel-space pilot, not a numerical reproduction of the latent-space benchmark. Appendix~\ref{app:video-case} gives the training and metric protocol.

Table~\ref{tab:video-main-fvd} shows a mixed quality--cost tradeoff. Eight It\^o evaluations reduce complete-ensemble generation time by $6.07\times$ on KTH and $5.90\times$ on CLEVRER relative to SI64, but pooled FVD rises from 773.5 to 888.2 and from 222.3 to 317.3, respectively: penalties of 14.8\% and 42.8\%. The KTH CRPS values are close (0.02857 versus 0.02853), whereas CLEVRER CRPS worsens by 17.1\%. Energy score and mean sample LPIPS also worsen on both datasets. Thus similar pixelwise CRPS on KTH does not establish preserved video quality; one-step It\^o sampling degrades FVD further on both datasets.

The eight-step deterministic map has the lowest observed KTH FVD (706.6), while SI64 gives the lowest CLEVRER FVD (222.3). Against FM64, FlowMap8 reduces latency by $7.39\times$ and $7.29\times$, with lower FVD but about 5.2--5.4\% higher CRPS. The competitive FM16 reference narrows those speedups to $1.86\times$ and $1.83\times$. Rankings therefore depend on dataset, metric and reference budget, rather than universally favoring more steps or stochastic maps.

Persistence gives the highest mean sample PSNR and SSIM on both datasets, yet its CLEVRER FVD is 1072.2 and its ensemble spread is zero. Image similarity alone can favor an unchanged background and pose without representing future motion. Figure~\ref{fig:video-main-sequences} displays fixed individual completions alongside the true frame sequences; examples were not selected by quality. Appendix~\ref{app:video-results} reports all proper scores, image metrics, dispersion and costs. These single-training, four-completion pilots establish measured runtime gains, not cross-seed robustness, equivalence or general sample-quality retention.
