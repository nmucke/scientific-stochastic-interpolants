# Interpretation of completed KTH pilot

This is a **KTH-only development report**, prepared after `../kth_audit.json` passed and while CLEVRER evaluation remained in progress. All five KTH families and all nine prespecified method/budget combinations are included. No canonical two-dataset manuscript comparison has been written from this subset.

The matched test contains 128 clips, four completions per clip, ten observed frames and thirty forecast frames at 64×64 grayscale resolution. Frame metrics average over leads with equal weight per original source video. Timings cover the complete four-member rollout. Pooled FVD uses 128 real and 512 generated clips; each member FVD uses 128 versus 128. These are pixel-space pilot results, not a reproduction of the published latent-space benchmark or its much larger completion budget. One training realization does not establish training-seed robustness or equivalence.

## Quality and cost

- **Eight-step Itô map versus its 64-step stochastic-interpolant teacher:** CRPS is 0.02857 versus 0.02853, a descriptive 0.12% increase, while rollout time falls from 12.58 to 2.071 seconds (6.07× faster). However, pooled FVD rises from 773.5 to 888.2 (14.8%), mean sample LPIPS rises from 0.1706 to 0.1931, and energy score rises from 0.04664 to 0.04767. The result supports close pixelwise probabilistic score at lower cost; it does **not** support unchanged perceptual or sequence quality. Close point estimates are not an equivalence test.
- **One-step Itô map:** 0.2765 seconds is much faster, but CRPS 0.03191, FVD 1271 and LPIPS 0.3487 are substantially worse than its teacher. It also loses to persistence on these three metrics. One-step sampling is not a successful general quality-preserving replacement in this run.
- **Eight-step deterministic flow map:** pooled FVD 706.6 is the best observed KTH point estimate, with member FVD 748.0 ± 19.0. It takes 1.673 seconds, versus flow matching's 3.115 seconds at 16 steps or 12.36 seconds at 64 steps. Nevertheless, CRPS 0.02774 is worse than both flow-matching values (0.02648 and 0.02636), and energy score 0.04666 is also worse. It provides a favorable measured sequence-quality/cost tradeoff, not uniform superiority. Its 7.39× speedup versus the 64-step teacher should be accompanied by the competitive 16-step reference; against that reference it is only 1.86× faster.
- **One-step deterministic flow map:** 0.2270 seconds gives a strong low-cost option, with CRPS 0.02760. Its FVD 857.2 and LPIPS 0.1913 are worse than the eight-step map. Its slightly better CRPS than the eight-step version does not imply better video quality. Relative to 64-step flow matching, speedup is 54.45× but CRPS is 4.69% worse and FVD 9.45% worse.
- **Solver sensitivity matters:** the stochastic interpolant at 16 steps deteriorates to CRPS 0.05265 and FVD 1782; 64 steps is much better. Conversely, 16-step flow matching has better FVD and mean sample LPIPS than its 64-step version, while 64 steps has slightly better CRPS. More integration steps are not a monotone quality improvement across metrics, and no run is established as an exact distributional reference.

These FVD rankings are point estimates on the fixed sample. Completion-member standard deviations are not confidence intervals, and their overlap or separation is not a significance test.

## Persistence and uncertainty

Persistence is a necessary strong baseline here: FVD 816.1, LPIPS 0.1650, PSNR 24.77 dB and SSIM 0.8049, at roughly 0.0038 seconds. It has the best sample PSNR and SSIM among the evaluated methods and lower LPIPS than most. However, its CRPS 0.03112 and energy score 0.06741 are worse than both flow-map budgets, both flow-matching budgets, the eight-step Itô map and the 64-step stochastic interpolant. It has zero spread by construction and cannot express uncertain motion. This metric disagreement is consistent with static backgrounds and short-term image similarity favoring repeated frames; the observation does not demonstrate accurate motion forecasting.

Mean corrected spread/skill ratios are below one for every generative method: about 0.714 for Itô8, 0.717 for SI64, 0.691 for FlowMap8 and 0.760 for FM64. These averages describe limited dispersion in this pilot; they are not a full conditional calibration test. Four-member empirical 5–95% interval coverage must not be interpreted as a nominal 90% calibrated interval. Image metrics clip display-range copies while CRPS, energy and calibration retain original values; the largest mean out-of-range fraction is about 0.00343 (FlowMap8), not zero.

## Fixed-case visual review

All nine generated GIF files were checked for their expected 40 frames. The same predetermined first case and members 0/1 are used throughout. The six main comparison GIFs were visually reviewed at forecast leads 1, 15 and 30; the remaining SI16, Itô1 and FM64 GIFs were also reviewed at lead 30. These frame inspections verify the saved animation content and do not replace a population-level assessment of motion coherence.

In this one fixed case, the models begin near the observed subject, but later frames show pose mismatch and altered background texture. Itô8 exhibits visible contrast/texture drift; SI16 and Itô1 show severe degradation by the final frame. FlowMap8 preserves a more recognizable subject but does not match the true final pose. Persistence preserves the last observed pose and background, which can look sharp while failing to generate the action's continuation. No alternative case was selected to improve appearance, and these observations must not be generalized as a dataset-wide ranking.

The accompanying `kth/lead_metrics.png` and `kth/fvd_vs_cost.png` were visually inspected: labels, costs and counts are consistent with `kth/summary.csv`. The completed KTH result supports a nuanced few-step tradeoff, with a particularly competitive deterministic map. It does not establish that the stochastic Itô map is the strongest video method, or that all quality measures are preserved by compression.
